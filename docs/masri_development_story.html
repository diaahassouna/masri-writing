How Masri Was Made

Before the website, the converter, and the framework documents, Masri was two handwritten pages. Here they are — the sketch that set the rules, and the manuscript that put them to the test.

From notebook to a live system

Those two pages turned into a full 37-letter alphabet spanning Latin, Coptic, and Greek letterforms, a Tier 2 academic ruleset, and a public framework released under CC BY 4.0. The alphabet now has a working Arabizi-to-Masri converter, keyboard layouts, and a reference site. The handwriting is still where all of it starts: every rule on this page traces back to a decision made with a pen, not a keyboard.
